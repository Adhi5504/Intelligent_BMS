Problem. Industrial centrifugal pumps are usually oversized for their real duty, so plants throttle them with a partly closed valve that burns the surplus head as heat. The waste stays hidden because flow, the variable that exposes it, is costly to measure: an energy-audit practitioner reports that wrong sizing is the most common cause and that 30–40% of ultrasonic flow readings go wrong. Electrical power and pressure, by contrast, are cheap to measure.

Why it matters. Throttling wastes electricity and CO2 every hour a pump runs, yet small plants (MSMEs) rarely get an audit, so owners cannot tell which pump justifies a variable-speed drive (VFD). A wrong VFD purchase costs ₹50,200–1,09,400, and a missed one wastes energy and CO2 every year.

Proposed solution. PumpRupee is a low-cost, clamp-on audit kit with a web dashboard. An ESP32 logger records real power (current clamp and voltage sensor) and pressures from existing gauge ports for 7–14 days, with no pipe cutting and no shutdown. The software fits a head scale and a power scale to the pump from the logs (profile likelihood), infers flow from the pump curve plus power and head, and forecasts the VFD saving by speed matching with the affinity laws. A Shapley fair-share method splits the waste among throttling, impeller wear and a clogged strainer, and a CUSUM test flags slow wear. The owner sees waste in rupees per year, SEC (kWh/m3), the cheapest fix first, the lowest safe VFD setpoint and a payback verdict for that specific pump. After the fix, the same kit re-measures to verify the saving. It audits and recommends; it never controls the pump.

Unique and innovative aspects. Pump monitoring and sensorless flow already exist (Altivar Process, KSB PumpMeter, Samotics). Our contribution is the integration for MSMEs: waste priced in rupees by cause, the priced value of an extra sensor, a shared kit costing about ₹6,100–13,300 with no flow meter, built-in before/after verification and a pump-specific VFD verdict before any capital spend. It complements Schneider drives and EcoStruxure by deciding which pumps deserve one.

Expected impact and beneficiaries. In simulation on a real Grundfos NB 65-160/157 (11 kW) pump, a VFD saves 19.3% (₹69,292 and 6.1 t CO2 per year) with constant pressure and 35.5% (₹1,27,178 and 11.3 t) with proportional pressure; the valve is 83–93% of the waste. Payback is 4–19 months for an oversized pump but 2–4+ years if right-sized, so the audit tells the owner which case applies. Forecast error is about ±3 percentage points with a measured system curve, otherwise about ±10. Beneficiaries are MSME plant owners, maintenance teams, energy auditors and drive channel partners.

Status and limits. A working dashboard is live at https://pumprupee-dashboard.vercel.app/ and can calibrate from an uploaded CSV. All results are simulated; there is no lab or field data yet. Next is a bench test against a reference flow meter.
