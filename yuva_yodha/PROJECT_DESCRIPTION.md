**PumpRupee: pricing pump energy waste in rupees** (Team ANS_4X · Smart Manufacturing)

Industrial pumps are usually oversized for their real duty, so plants throttle them with a partly closed valve. The energy burnt across that valve is invisible to the owner because flow is hard and expensive to measure. An energy-audit practitioner told us wrong sizing is the most common cause of throttling and that 30–40% of ultrasonic flow readings go wrong. Full audits rarely reach small plants.

PumpRupee is a low-cost, clamp-on audit kit with a web dashboard. An ESP32 logger records motor power from a clamp-on sensor and one to three pressures taken from existing gauge ports,. After 7–14 days of logs, the software calibrates the pump to its real condition and infers flow from the pump curve plus power and head, so no flow meter is needed. It then reports the ₹ wasted per year, the waste split by cause (throttle valve, impeller wear, clogged strainer) using a Shapley fair-share method, the cheapest fix first, the lowest safe VFD setpoint, and whether a VFD will pay back for that specific pump. A CUSUM test watches for slow impeller wear. After a fix, the same kit re-measures to verify the saving. PumpRupee audits and recommends; it never controls the pump.

It fits Smart Manufacturing directly. It makes motor energy visible (Electrify), diagnoses waste and wear automatically (Automate), and turns logs into a ₹ business case with before/after proof (Digitalise). It complements Schneider's Altivar Process drives and EcoStruxure: PumpRupee finds which pumps justify a drive, builds the case an MSME owner understands, and verifies the saving afterwards.

Results are simulated on the real Grundfos NB 65-160/157 (11 kW) curves. With 10 m static lift, 16 h/day, 300 days a year and ₹8/kWh, a VFD saves 19.3% (₹69,292 and 6.1 t CO₂ per year) with constant pressure and 35.5% (₹1,27,178 and 11.3 t CO₂) with proportional pressure. The valve accounts for 83–93% of the waste. Payback is 4–19 months for an oversized pump but 2–4+ years for a right-sized, high-lift pump, so the audit tells the owner which case they are in before they spend ₹50,200–1,09,400. The kit costs about ₹6,100–13,300 (indicative prices).

Pump monitoring, sensorless flow and VFD savings already exist (KSB PumpMeter, US DOE PSAT, Samotics, Altivar Process). Our contribution is the integration for MSMEs: ₹ by cause, pricing the value of an extra sensor, a shared kit with no flow meter, built-in verification, and a pump-specific VFD verdict.

A working prototype dashboard is live at https://pumprupee-dashboard.vercel.app/ and can calibrate from an uploaded CSV of power and pressure logs. All results are simulated: there is no lab or field data yet. Forecasts are accurate to about ±3 percentage points only with a measured system curve, otherwise about ±10. Next steps are a bench test with a reference flow meter, a pilot at one or two plants, and validation of the forecast error.
